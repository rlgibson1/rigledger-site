    IFTA, explained plainly

    Who has to file IFTA?

    You need an IFTA license if you run a qualified motor vehicle in two or more member jurisdictions: the 48 contiguous states and the 10 Canadian provinces. A qualified motor vehicle has two axles and a gross or registered weight over 26,000 lb, or three or more axles at any weight, or runs in a combination over 26,000 lb (IFTA Articles of Agreement, R245). You file one return a quarter with your base state, and it settles with the others.

    How is IFTA calculated?

    Each state gets tax on the fuel you burned there, at its own rate. Fleet MPG is your total miles divided by your total gallons for the quarter. A state's taxable gallons are your taxable miles there divided by fleet MPG. Subtract the tax-paid gallons you bought in that state and multiply what's left by its rate. A positive number means you owe that state and a negative one is a credit. Credits offset what you owe other states on the same return (R1120.200). If the whole return comes out as a credit, it carries forward to later returns for up to eight quarters unless you ask your base state for a refund (R1110, R1120.100).

    Which gallons go on a state's line?

    Only tax-paid gallons: fuel that went into the truck's tank with that state's fuel tax in the price, from a pump or from your own tax-paid bulk tank there (R1010.100, R1020.200). On the return that column is "Tax paid volume" (Procedures Manual P720.500.030). Fuel with no tax paid on it, such as untaxed bulk fuel, earns no credit in any state, so leave it off the state lines and add it to the total gallons under "My totals are bigger than these lines". Fleet MPG still counts it.

    What is fleet MPG, and which miles count?

    Fleet MPG is your total miles in every jurisdiction divided by the total gallons you put in the truck's fuel tanks, for the whole quarter. Every mile counts, deadhead and personal miles included. The calculator treats the miles on each state's line as taxable there. If you also ran miles outside IFTA (Alaska, Washington, D.C., the Canadian territories, or Mexico), or a state exempts some of your miles, leave those off the state lines, open "My totals are bigger than these lines" and type your real totals, because fleet MPG uses all of them. Fuel for a separate reefer tank isn't fuel that moves the truck, so leave it out.

    Why do Kentucky and Virginia cost more?

    On the 3rd Quarter 2026 matrix they add a surcharge on top of the fuel tax: taxable gallons times the surcharge rate. Fuel you buy in those states earns no credit against it, so the surcharge is owed even if every gallon you burned there was bought there. Indiana's surcharge line is $0 on this quarter's matrix.

    Why does Oregon show $0?

    Oregon has no IFTA fuel tax rate on diesel. It collects a weight-mile tax separately through ODOT. Enter your Oregon miles and gallons anyway, because they count toward fleet MPG.

    What does a negative number mean?

    It's a credit. You bought more fuel in that state than you burned there, so you already paid it more tax at the pump than your miles there owe. Credits offset what you owe other states on the same return.

    When is IFTA due?

    On the last day of the month after each quarter ends: 1st quarter (January to March) by April 30, 2nd (April to June) by July 31, 3rd (July to September) by October 31, and 4th (October to December) by January 31. If that day is a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the next business day is the due date (R960.100). That's why the 3rd quarter 2026 return is due Monday, November 2, 2026 (October 31 is a Saturday) and the 4th quarter 2026 return is due Monday, February 1, 2027 (January 31 is a Sunday). A quarter with no miles still needs a return while your license is active (R930.100).

    What should I keep in case of an audit?

    Keep everything for four years from the due date or the date you filed, whichever is later (IFTA Procedures Manual, P510). For each trip: start and end dates, origin and destination, route, beginning and ending odometer readings, total miles, miles in each jurisdiction, and the unit number (P540.100). For each fuel purchase: a receipt showing the date, the seller's name and address, gallons, fuel type, price, the unit it went into, and the buyer's name (P550.300). An altered, erased, or unreadable receipt earns no credit unless you can show it's valid (P550.200), and thermal paper fades, so photograph receipts the day you get them. If an ELD or GPS keeps your miles, check that it exports the position records as a spreadsheet or CSV; a PDF report doesn't count (P540.200).

    It's the same fleet-MPG math the return uses, run on the numbers you type, so it's only as good as your miles and receipts. Your base state's online filing may differ by a few cents, and its return or portal does the official calculation. Use this to check your numbers and know where you stand before you file.
