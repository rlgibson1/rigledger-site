How does the odometer method work?

Your state miles come from your odometer, not from GPS tracking. You tap STATE LINE when you cross, and you read the odometer when you're parked. When you save a reading, the app suggests the odometer number at each state line you crossed since the last reading, splitting the miles by the distance along your route (or by time, when it has no locations). You tap OK or Edit on each one.

Suggested numbers are marked "est." on the trip record (and "est. (unconfirmed)" if you never tapped OK), so anyone reading it can tell a typed reading from an estimate. The best readings to take: when you park near a state line, at each fuel stop, and on the first day of each quarter.

What do I do at a state line?

Tap STATE LINE once. That's all. The crossing is saved right away, and the app shows the new state with an Undo button for a few seconds. It never asks you for anything at the line. The odometer for that crossing goes on the to-do list, and the next time you enter a reading when you're parked, the app suggests the number at the line for you to OK or edit.

No signal, location turned off, or nowhere near a border? The crossing is still saved, with no miles lost, and the to-do list asks you to pick the state when you're parked.

For the best state suggestions, allow Precise location for RigLedger IFTA in your phone's settings. With approximate location only, the app can name the wrong state near a border, so check the state it shows.

Why does the app never ask me to type while I'm driving?

Federal rules for commercial drivers treat more than one button press on a phone as hand-held use, and typing as texting. So while you're moving, the app only ever asks for one tap: STATE LINE. The number pad appears only on screens you open yourself, like FUEL and ODOMETER, which are for when you're parked. That's our reading of the rules, not legal advice.

When do new rates arrive?

IFTA, Inc. publishes the official diesel rates for each quarter, and some quarters aren't final until weeks after the quarter starts. We post them to this site once they're published, and the app picks them up free the next time you open it with a signal (it checks at most once a day). Until then, the app uses the latest rates it has and says so on the Return screen and the PDF, because your total may change.

What is "Type my totals"?

For drivers who already have their numbers, from an ELD state-mileage report or from paper. Open the Return tab, pick the quarter, and choose Type my totals. Enter each state with its miles and tax-paid gallons. If you drove miles outside IFTA or bought fuel without tax, add your overall total miles and gallons too. Your entries are saved per quarter.

How do I restore my purchase on a new phone?

The unlock belongs to your Apple ID or Google account, not the phone. Sign in to the new phone with the same account, install the app, and go to More, then Restore purchase. You don't pay again.

How do I back up, or move to a new phone?

Go to More, then Back up and restore. There are two kinds of backup file:

  • Back up a quarter saves that quarter's entries with their receipt photos, plus your typed totals and driver details.
  • Back up everything saves every entry and typed total in one small file, without photos.

Either one opens your phone's share sheet, so you can save the file to your files or cloud drive, or email it to yourself.

To move to a new phone, back up everything, plus each quarter whose receipt photos you want to keep. On the new phone, install the app and answer the two start questions, then go to More, Back up and restore, Restore from a file, and choose each file in turn. Nothing on the phone is deleted, newer changes win, and restoring the same file twice changes nothing.

Backup and restore are free. IFTA asks you to keep your records for four years, and the app keeps everything until you delete it, so back up each quarter when it ends.

Does it need a signal or an account?

No to both. Everything works offline, and there's no sign-up. The only download is the public rates file. See the app's privacy policy.

What the app doesn't do

  • File your return. It gives you the numbers and a PDF to file from; you file with your base state.
  • Track GPS in the background. It reads your location only when you tap STATE LINE, FUEL or ODOMETER, and once during setup to suggest your starting state.
  • Fleets. It's built for one truck.
  • Canada-based filers (liters and Canadian dollars).
  • Gasoline or other fuels. It carries diesel rates only.
